#### 1. Read the following passage carefully: [12 Marks]

1. Heritage conservation is often mistakenly viewed as an elite indulgence—a nostalgic effort to freeze antiquity in glass showcases. However, authentic living heritage is not a static museum relic; it is an active ecological, social, and architectural memory that binds communities to their civilizational geography. Across historical towns in India, indigenous architecture, vernacular water harvesting systems, and traditional urban squares (*chowks*) evolved over centuries as climatic adaptations. They represented sustainable living systems refined through generations of experiential knowledge.

2. When rapid urbanization systematically bulldozes these heritage fabrics to replace them with uniform concrete blocks and energy-hungry glass facades, the loss extends far beyond aesthetics. The destruction of historic stepwells (*baolis*) and interconnected urban tanks, for instance, has crippled natural groundwater recharge mechanisms in cities like Delhi, Jodhpur, and Hyderabad. Similarly, traditional courtyard houses with thick lime-plastered walls provided passive thermal comfort, maintaining interior temperatures up to 8°C cooler than ambient summer heat without consuming a single watt of electricity.

3. The neglect of traditional arts, crafts, and architectural restoration also fractures local economic ecosystems. Heritage restoration is labour-intensive and craft-dependent. Unlike modern mechanized construction, which funnels wealth into corporate supply chains, heritage preservation directly sustains master stonemasons, woodcarvers, lime-plaster artisans, and regional handicraft guilds. Revitalizing historic precincts transforms neglected quarters into vibrant cultural hubs, generating sustainable livelihoods through heritage walks, artisanal markets, and community-led hospitality.

4. To bridge the widening chasm between preservation and modern development, planners must embrace the concept of 'adaptive reuse'. Historic structures should not remain sealed monuments; they must be thoughtfully repurposed into modern libraries, vocational workshops, community health centres, and creative incubation spaces while preserving their architectural integrity. By anchoring future growth within the living roots of our heritage, societies can cultivate environmental resilience, social cohesion, and cultural dignity.
