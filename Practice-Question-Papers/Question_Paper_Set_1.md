#### 1. Read the following passage carefully: [12 Marks]

1. Philosophy of education is largely the study of the purpose, nature, and ideals of education. It is intimately related to the study of human development and human nature. The fundamental question that has engaged philosophers from Socrates to John Dewey is whether education is merely a preparation for adult life or an essential component of life itself. When education is conceived solely as a preparatory process, it risks becoming rigid, utilitarian, and detached from the immediate psychological and emotional needs of the developing individual. 

2. True education, as thinkers like Rabindranath Tagore and Jiddu Krishnamurti observed, is fundamentally holistic. It does not consist in stuffing the young mind with disconnected facts and vocational algorithms, but in awakening an enduring curiosity, a critical intellect, and a compassionate sensibility. The classroom should not be a microcosm of industrialized efficiency, but a sanctuary of creative discovery where failure is seen as a necessary stepping stone to insight.

3. In contemporary times, the rapid proliferation of artificial intelligence and algorithmic learning systems has brought a renewed urgency to this debate. While automated platforms can deliver factual knowledge with unprecedented precision, they cannot cultivate empathy, moral courage, or aesthetic discernment. An education that neglects these dimensions creates individuals who are technically proficient yet ethically anchorless.

4. Furthermore, education must foster an intimate relationship between the child and nature. When children are insulated from the living rhythms of the natural world, their capacity for wonder atrophies. To sit under a tree, observe the geometry of a leaf, or listen to the cadence of the rain is as vital to cognitive flowering as mathematical computation. An authentic educational paradigm harmonizes cognitive rigour with ecological consciousness and ethical integrity.

The surveyed sample comprised 12,000 students from both private and government institutions. 

2. The findings revealed a stark shift in student engagement with long-form print literature versus short-form digital media. In 2023, approximately 44% of students reported reading physical books (excluding school textbooks) for at least 30 minutes daily. By 2025, this proportion had dropped to 21%. Conversely, daily time dedicated to short-form video feeds and interactive social messaging rose precipitously from an average of 85 minutes per day in 2023 to 175 minutes per day in 2025.

**Table 1: Student Time Allocation & Cognitive Focus Metrics (2023–2025)**

| Metric Evaluated | 2023 Survey (%) | 2024 Survey (%) | 2025 Survey (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |

| Sustained Attention Span (>45 mins without distraction) | 52% | 37% | 24% |
| Self-reported Vocabulary Retention Score (out of 100) | 78 | 69 | 61 |

Students maintaining habitual print reading exhibited higher critical analysis scores and greater patience with intricate textual arguments.
