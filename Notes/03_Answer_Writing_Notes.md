## 1. OFFICIAL MARKING SCHEME & QUESTION TAXONOMY (1, 2, 4, 5 & 6 MARKS)

The CBSE Class 12 English Core examination evaluates four core competencies: **Content**, **Organisation of Ideas**, **Expression**, and **Accuracy of Spelling & Grammar**. Class 12 English Core has **0 direct standalone grammar questions**; grammar is evaluated strictly under Accuracy (1 mark each in Questions 3 to 6, and within the expression rubrics of Section C).

### Summary Rubrics by Question Weightage

| Question Type | Questions in Paper | Word Limit | Official CBSE Mark Breakdown | Evaluator Priority |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1-Mark Questions** | Q1, Q2, Q7, Q8, Q9 | 1 sentence / 1 word / Option | **1 Mark:** Correct Key (MCQ) or complete value point (Objective/Inference). | Zero tolerance for ambiguity. In MCQs, write Option Letter + full option text. |
| **2-Mark Questions** | Q1, Q2 (Reading) <br> Q10 (Flamingo) <br> Q11 (Vistas) | 40–50 words (3–4 sentences) | **Reading:** 1m + 1m for dual reasons/points. <br> **Literature:** Content = 1m, Expression = 1m (½–1m deducted for spelling/grammar errors). | Must state 2 clear value points. Avoid plot retelling; answer the direct prompt. |
| **4-Mark Questions** | Q3 (Notice) <br> Q4 (Invitations/Replies) | Strictly within 50 words | **Format:** 1 mark <br> **Content:** 2 marks <br> **Accuracy:** 1 mark | Enclose in a clean box. Missing 1–2 format elements = -½ mark; >2 elements = 0 format marks. |
| **5-Mark Questions** | Q5 (Letters) <br> Q6 (Article/Report) <br> Q12 & Q13 (Literature LA) | 120–150 words (3 structured paragraphs) | **Writing (Q5 & Q6):** <br> • Format = 1m <br> • Organisation = 1m <br> • Content = 2m <br> • Accuracy = 1m <br><br> **Literature (Q12 & Q13):** <br> • Content = 2m <br> • Expression = 2m <br> • Accuracy = 1m | Coherence, seamless paragraph transitions, thematic insight, and textual vocabulary. | **6-Mark Questions** | Q7 (Poetry Extract) | Extract sub-questions (6 x 1m) | **6 sub-questions x 1 Mark each** = 6 Marks total. | Exact identification of poetic devices, contextual meanings, and thematic contrast. |

### B. SECTION C: LITERATURE (40 MARKS)

#### QUESTION 7: POETRY EXTRACT WALKTHROUGH (6 MARKS)

**Extract:**
> *"When Aunt is dead, her terrified hands will lie*  
> *Still ringed with ordeals she was mastered by.*  
> *The tigers in the panel that she made*  
> *Will go on prancing, proud and unafraid."*

- **Sub-Q (i): What is the symbolic significance of Aunt Jennifer's "terrified hands" remaining "ringed with ordeals" even after death? [1]**  
  **100% Model Answer:** The "terrified hands" symbolize the permanent psychological trauma and patriarchal subjugation Aunt Jennifer suffered. The word "ringed" represents both the physical entrapment of her oppressive wedding band and the endless cycle of domestic ordeals that bind her even in death.
- **Sub-Q (ii): Which literary device is employed in the phrase "ringed with ordeals"? [1]**  
  **100% Model Answer:** (B) Pun (The word "ringed" functions as a pun—denoting the circular gold wedding band on her finger, and metaphorically referring to being surrounded, trapped, or fettered by oppressive marital burdens).
- **Sub-Q (iii): Complete the sentence: [1]**  
  *The striking contrast between Aunt Jennifer's subjugation and the tigers' immortality illustrates that while the artist perishes under oppression, her art...*  
  **100% Model Answer:** ...survives eternally as an enduring, fearless testament to her suppressed desire for freedom and autonomy.
- **Sub-Q (iv): What qualities do the tigers possess that Aunt Jennifer was denied throughout her married life? [1]**  
  **100% Model Answer:** The tigers possess chivalric courage, majestic freedom, pride, and fearlessness—attributes of self-determination denied to Aunt Jennifer by patriarchal domination.
- **Sub-Q (v): State whether TRUE or FALSE: [1]**  
  *The wedding ring represented an emancipating bond of mutual respect and spiritual freedom for Aunt Jennifer.*  
  **100% Model Answer:** FALSE.
- **Sub-Q (vi): What does the eternal "prancing" of the tigers signify in the context of human creative expression? [1]**  
  **100% Model Answer:** It signifies that artistic creation transcends mortal vulnerabilities; art outlives the creator, preserving their deepest spiritual yearnings for liberty across generations.

**Q: What is the "childish longing in vain" that Robert Frost laments on behalf of the roadside vendors? [2]**

It is "childish" and "in vain" because the self-absorbed city motorists pass callously without sparing a penny, leaving the rural poor impoverished and neglected.

#### QUESTIONS 12 & 13: LONG LITERATURE ANSWERS (5 MARKS | 120–150 WORDS)
*CBSE Evaluation Rule: Content = 2 marks, Expression = 2 marks, Accuracy = 1 mark.*

##### Model Answer 1 (Comparative Literature HOTS: Keats vs. Frost | 5 Marks)
**Q: Both John Keats in *A Thing of Beauty* and Robert Frost in *A Roadside Stand* contemplate human suffering—one through romantic transcendentalism, the other through grim socio-economic realism. Contrast their perspectives on the human condition. [5]**

**100% Model Answer:**
In *A Thing of Beauty*, John Keats approaches human suffering from a romantic, transcendental perspective. Acknowledging that earthly life is marred by "despondence", "inhuman dearth of noble natures", and "gloomy days", Keats posits that encounters with enduring shapes of natural and artistic beauty—such as daffodils, clear rills, and noble epics of the mighty dead—dispel the dark pall from our spirits, offering an immortal source of joy and spiritual rejuvenation.

Conversely, Robert Frost’s *A Roadside Stand* strips away romantic idealism to confront raw socio-economic destitution. Frost depicts the impoverished rural folk whose survival depends not on abstract contemplation of nature, but on the tangible "flow of city cash". While Keats’ nature heals the soul, Frost reveals how systemic neglect and the callous indifference of the urban elite crush the rural spirit under deceptive promises of development.

Thus, while Keats celebrates beauty as a universal, eternal balm that transcends earthly misery, Frost urgently demonstrates that aesthetic appreciation cannot replace economic justice; spiritual solace remains hollow when fundamental material dignity is denied.

##### Model Answer 2 (Vistas Character Dilemma: Dr. Sadao in *The Enemy* | 5 Marks)
**Q: Dr. Sadao’s internal conflict between patriotic duty and universal human compassion forms the moral core of *The Enemy*. Discuss how Sadao’s actions resolve this dilemma. [5]**

**100% Model Answer:**
In Pearl S. Buck's *The Enemy*, Dr. Sadao Hoki is confronted with an agonizing moral impasse when a critically wounded American prisoner of war, Tom, washes ashore near his secluded coastal home. As a patriotic Japanese subject during World War II, harboring or aiding an enemy combatant constituted treason, punishable by execution and familial dishonour.

However, Sadao’s medical conditioning in America and the Hippocratic oath compelled him to prioritize human life above narrow geopolitical allegiances. Despite the open defiance and abandonment of his domestic servants, Sadao meticulously operates on the bleeding soldier, extracting the bullet near the kidney with masterly surgical precision. He shelters and nurses Tom back to health, supported by Hana’s resilient courage.

Sadao’s ultimate moral victory lies in orchestrating Tom’s nocturnal escape to an uninhabited island with rations, fresh water, and signaling instructions. By ensuring the American's survival while maintaining his strategic loyalty to the General, Sadao proves that universal humanitarian compassion transcends nationalistic borders and wartime prejudice.

## 3. THE EXAMINER'S RED PEN: 10 FATAL MISTAKES THAT COST MARKS

1. **The Notice & Invitation Box Trap:** Forgetting to draw a pencil box around Notices (Q3) and Invitations/Replies (Q4). Evaluators deduct **1 mark** immediately under Format.
2. **The "Yours Sincerely" in Letters to Editor:** Using *"Yours faithfully"* or *"Yours sincerely"* in a Letter to the Editor. The **only** universally accepted CBSE closing is **"Yours truly"**. Use *"Yours sincerely"* exclusively in Job Applications.
3. **Missing the Enclosed Bio-Data Table:** In Job Applications, writing a narrative paragraph instead of a separate, tabulated Curriculum Vitae with columns for Examination, Board/University, Year, and Division. Evaluators dock 1.5 marks for missing bio-data format elements.
4. **Exceeding the 50-Word Limit:** Writing 80–100 words in a Notice or Invitation. CBSE penalizes word limit violations under the Expression and Format parameters. Keep notices crisp, factual, and strictly within 50 words.
5. **Plot Retelling in 2-Mark Literature Questions:** Summarizing the entire chapter instead of targeting the specific question. If asked *why* M. Hamel wrote on the blackboard, answering with the Prussian conquest of Alsace wastes words and loses the Expression mark. State the 2 value points directly.
6. **Incomplete MCQ Answers:** Writing only the option letter (e.g., "(A)") or only the text without the option letter. The official CBSE answer key prescribes: **"(A) Fostering intellectual curiosity"**. Both must be present.
7. **Tense Inconsistency in Report Writing:** Writing a School Event Report in present tense or future tense. A report narrates an event that has *already concluded*; it must strictly employ the **past tense** and **passive voice** (e.g., *"The conclave was inaugurated..."* not *"The conclave inaugurates..."*).
8. **Omitting Byline in Articles and Reports:** Failing to write *"By [Name]"* directly beneath the title/headline. This forfeits ½ mark of the Format component immediately.
9. **Superficial Vistas Long Answers:** Failing to quote or analyze core psychological symbols in Vistas (e.g., omitting the "first-day cover" in *The Third Level*, the "wooden toy tiger" in *The Tiger King*, or the "acid burn vs. tin leg" contrast in *On the Face of It*).
10. **Misattributing Authors and Poets:** Misspelling or swapping poet names (e.g., confusing John Keats with Robert Frost, or attributing *Aunt Jennifer's Tigers* to Sylvia Plath instead of Adrienne Rich).
